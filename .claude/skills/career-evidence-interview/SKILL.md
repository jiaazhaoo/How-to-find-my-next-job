# Interview

Every stage before this one can only rearrange what the artifacts already
contain. This one produces something new — the person's answers — and one
class of answer in particular that no repository can ever supply:

> A repository can prove someone is good at something.
> It can never show whether they still want to do it.

## Input

`workspace/07_questions.json`. Each question carries:

- `kind` — what kind of hole in the record provoked it
- `observation` — what the record actually shows
- `resolves` — what an answer would settle
- `angle` + `angle_meaning` — which Career Construction Interview purpose it
  serves; this tells you what to listen for, and what to follow up on
- `card_ids` — the evidence that produced the question
- `draft` — a serviceable phrasing, **not** the question to read out

## Before you start

Read the cards behind each question (`workspace/05_cards.jsonl`, matching
`card_ids`). You are about to ask someone about their own work; showing up
without having read it is both rude and produces worse questions.

Then say, in two sentences, what this is: seven-ish questions drawn from
places where their own record is thin or contradicts itself, and their answers
become evidence like anything else.

## Asking

**Rephrase every draft.** The drafts are generated from templates and read
like it. Rewrite each one using the specific material — the actual project,
the actual numbers, the actual dates — in the language the person writes in.
"关于 migration：你的记录很扎实" is a template. "支付网关那次双写迁移你从头
做到尾，P99 也压下来了" is a question someone will actually engage with.

**One at a time.** This is a conversation, not a form. Ask, listen, respond to
what they said, then move on. Never paste all seven at once.

**Show your reasoning when it helps.** People answer better when they can see
why you are asking: "我看到四条证据都指向你主导了迁移，但没有一处显示你在没人
要求的时候碰它" invites a real answer. Do not do this for every question — it
gets tiresome — but do it when the observation is the interesting part.

**Follow up at most once per question**, and only when the answer is thin or
raises something sharper than the original question. The `angle` tells you
where to push:

| angle | listen for | good follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| `self_concept` | how they describe themselves vs what the record shows | "那个说法和材料里的 X 对不上，哪个更接近实际？" |
| `environment` | what kind of problem or team they gravitate to | "那种环境里最吸引你的是哪部分？" |
| `script` | how they think change happens | "如果重来一次，你会在哪一步换个做法？" |
| `advice` | the rule they would give someone else | "你会提醒接手的人注意什么？" |
| `preoccupation` | what is unresolved for them now | "这件事现在还让你不舒服吗？" |

**Never argue with an answer.** If someone says they do not want to do
migrations any more, that is the finding — not a position to test. You may ask
what changed. You may not try to talk them out of it.

**Stop early if they are done.** Four good answers beat seven rushed ones.

## Recording

Write one JSONL line per answer, then hand it to the pipeline:

```json
{"question_id":"q1","kind":"interest","claim":"明确不想再把迁移当主线，但愿意兜底","answer":"<their words, verbatim>","skills":["migration"],"role_signal":"owner","confidence":0.9}
```

```bash
career-evidence answers --file /path/to/answers.jsonl
```

Two fields decide whether this helps or pollutes:

1. **`answer` must be verbatim.** It becomes the quote that `career-evidence verify`
   checks. Paraphrasing breaks the check and, worse, replaces their words with
   yours in their own profile.
2. **`kind` must match what they actually said.** Describing work they did →
   `capability` / `decision` / `impact` (an `impact` claim needs a number).
   Saying what they want or enjoy → `interest`. Describing themselves →
   `self_concept`, which can never become a capability pattern. Marking a
   preference as a capability quietly inflates the evidence layer, and nothing
   downstream can detect it.

`claim` is your one-line summary and may be your words; `answer` may not.

## After

`career-evidence answers` reports which themes their testimony corroborated — an
anecdote can become a pattern this way, because their word is a genuinely
independent source. Tell them what changed, then point at
`career-evidence profile`.
